📊 Law of Total Probability

Computing unconditional probabilities by conditioning on a partition of the sample space

Probability Theory · Unit II · Total Probability Theorem
📜

1. Introduction

The Law of Total Probability (also called the Theorem of Total Probability) is a fundamental result in probability theory that expresses the probability of an event in terms of its conditional probabilities given a partition of the sample space. It is the foundation of Bayesian inference and is used extensively in applications ranging from medical testing to machine learning.

If we have a set of mutually exclusive and exhaustive events \(B_1, B_2, \ldots, B_n\) that cover the entire sample space, then the probability of any event \(A\) can be computed as the weighted average of the conditional probabilities \(P(A \mid B_i)\), with weights \(P(B_i)\).

📐

2. Partition of the Sample Space

📌 Definition (Partition)

A collection of events \(\{B_1, B_2, \ldots, B_n\}\) is said to form a partition of the sample space \(\Omega\) if:

  • Mutually Exclusive: \(B_i \cap B_j = \emptyset\) for all \(i \neq j\)
  • Exhaustive: \(\displaystyle \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} B_i = \Omega\)
  • Non-zero Probability: \(P(B_i) > 0\) for all \(i\) (ensuring conditional probabilities are defined)

3. Law of Total Probability (Finite Partition)

📌 Theorem (Law of Total Probability for Finite Partition)

Let \(\{B_1, B_2, \ldots, B_n\}\) be a partition of the sample space \(\Omega\) with \(P(B_i) > 0\) for all \(i\). Then for any event \(A\),

\[ P(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} P(A \mid B_i) \cdot P(B_i) \]

📝 Proof:

Step 1 — Express event A as a union of disjoint parts:

Since the \(B_i\) form a partition of \(\Omega\), we have:

\[ A = A \cap \Omega = A \cap \left( \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} B_i \right) = \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} (A \cap B_i) \]
Step 2 — Note that the sets \(A \cap B_i\) are mutually exclusive:

Because the \(B_i\) are mutually exclusive, \((A \cap B_i) \cap (A \cap B_j) = \emptyset\) for \(i \neq j\).

Step 3 — Apply finite additivity (Axiom 3):
\[ P(A) = P\left( \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} (A \cap B_i) \right) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} P(A \cap B_i) \]
Step 4 — Apply the multiplication rule (definition of conditional probability):
\[ P(A \cap B_i) = P(A \mid B_i) \cdot P(B_i) \]
Step 5 — Substitute and conclude:
\[ P(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} P(A \mid B_i) \cdot P(B_i) \]
Step 6 — Conclusion:
\[ \boxed{P(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} P(A \mid B_i) P(B_i)} \]
📌 Corollary 3.1 (Special Case n = 2): For any event A and any event B with \(0 < P(B) < 1\),
\[ P(A) = P(A \mid B)P(B) + P(A \mid B^c)P(B^c) \]
📌 Corollary 3.2 (Weighted Average Interpretation): \(P(A)\) is a weighted average of the conditional probabilities \(P(A \mid B_i)\), with weights \(P(B_i)\). 📌 Corollary 3.3: \(\min_i P(A \mid B_i) \leq P(A) \leq \max_i P(A \mid B_i)\). The unconditional probability lies between the smallest and largest conditional probabilities.
∞

4. Law of Total Probability for Countably Infinite Partition

📌 Theorem (Countable Partition)

Let \(\{B_1, B_2, B_3, \ldots\}\) be a countable partition of the sample space \(\Omega\) with \(P(B_i) > 0\) for all \(i\). Then for any event \(A\),

\[ P(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(A \mid B_i) \cdot P(B_i) \]

📝 Proof:

Step 1 — Same as finite case, but using countable additivity:
\[ A = \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} (A \cap B_i) \]
Step 2 — Using countable additivity (Extended Axiom of Addition):
\[ P(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(A \cap B_i) \]
Step 3 — Apply multiplication rule:
\[ P(A \cap B_i) = P(A \mid B_i) P(B_i) \]
Step 4 — Conclusion:
\[ \boxed{P(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(A \mid B_i) P(B_i)} \]
📌 Corollary 4.1: The series \(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(A \mid B_i) P(B_i)\) converges absolutely because it equals \(P(A) \leq 1\).

📌 Corollary 4.2: If only finitely many \(B_i\) have positive probability, the infinite sum reduces to a finite sum.
∫

5. Law of Total Probability for Continuous Random Variables

📌 Theorem (Continuous Version)

If \(X\) is a continuous random variable with probability density function \(f_X(x)\), then for any event \(A\),

\[ P(A) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} P(A \mid X = x) \, f_X(x) \, dx \]

7. Worked Examples

📖 Example 1: Two Machines Factory
A factory has two machines. Machine 1 produces 60% of the total output with a defect rate of 2%. Machine 2 produces 40% of the total output with a defect rate of 3%. What is the probability that a randomly selected product is defective?

Solution: Let D = "product is defective", \(M_1\) = "from Machine 1", \(M_2\) = "from Machine 2".
\(P(M_1) = 0.6\), \(P(M_2) = 0.4\)
\(P(D \mid M_1) = 0.02\), \(P(D \mid M_2) = 0.03\)
By the Law of Total Probability:
\[ P(D) = P(D \mid M_1)P(M_1) + P(D \mid M_2)P(M_2) \]
\[ P(D) = 0.02 \times 0.6 + 0.03 \times 0.4 = 0.012 + 0.012 = 0.024 \]
So the overall defect probability is 2.4%.
📖 Example 2: Three Urns
Urn 1 contains 5 red and 3 blue balls. Urn 2 contains 4 red and 4 blue balls. Urn 3 contains 2 red and 6 blue balls. An urn is chosen uniformly at random, and then a ball is drawn from that urn. What is the probability of drawing a red ball?

Solution: Let \(R\) = "red ball", \(U_i\) = "choose urn i".
\(P(U_i) = \frac{1}{3}\) for \(i = 1, 2, 3\).
\(P(R \mid U_1) = \frac{5}{8}\), \(P(R \mid U_2) = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}\), \(P(R \mid U_3) = \frac{2}{8} = \frac{1}{4}\)
By Total Probability:
\[ P(R) = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{5}{8} + \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{3} \left( \frac{5}{8} + \frac{4}{8} + \frac{2}{8} \right) = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{11}{8} = \frac{11}{24} \approx 0.4583 \]
📖 Example 3: Medical Test
A disease affects 1% of the population. A test has 95% sensitivity and 90% specificity. Find the probability that a randomly selected person tests positive.

Solution: Let D = "has disease", T = "tests positive".
\(P(D) = 0.01\), \(P(D^c) = 0.99\)
\(P(T \mid D) = 0.95\) (true positive rate)
\(P(T \mid D^c) = 0.10\) (false positive rate, since specificity = 0.90)
\[ P(T) = P(T \mid D)P(D) + P(T \mid D^c)P(D^c) = 0.95 \times 0.01 + 0.10 \times 0.99 = 0.0095 + 0.099 = 0.1085 \]
So about 10.85% of the population tests positive.
🔄

8. Connection to Bayes' Theorem

The Law of Total Probability provides the denominator in Bayes' Theorem:
\[ P(B_i \mid A) = \frac{P(A \mid B_i) P(B_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^{n} P(A \mid B_j) P(B_j)} \]

Thus, the Law of Total Probability is essential for Bayesian updating.

9. Alternative Forms

📌 Form 1 (Using Expectation)

\[ P(A) = E[P(A \mid X)] \]

where \(X\) is any discrete random variable forming a partition.

📌 Form 2 (Continuous Case)

\[ P(A) = \int P(A \mid X = x) f_X(x) \, dx \]

10. Practice Problems

Problem 1: In a certain population, 30% are smokers. Among smokers, the probability of developing lung cancer is 0.15; among non-smokers, it is 0.02. What is the probability that a randomly selected person develops lung cancer?

Show Solution

\(P(C) = 0.15 \times 0.3 + 0.02 \times 0.7 = 0.045 + 0.014 = 0.059\) (5.9%)

Problem 2: A bag contains 3 red and 4 blue balls. Two balls are drawn without replacement. Find the probability that the second ball is red using total probability.

Show Solution

Let \(R_1\) = first is red, \(R_2\) = second is red. \(P(R_2) = P(R_2 \mid R_1)P(R_1) + P(R_2 \mid R_1^c)P(R_1^c) = \frac{2}{6} \times \frac{3}{7} + \frac{3}{6} \times \frac{4}{7} = \frac{6}{42} + \frac{12}{42} = \frac{18}{42} = \frac{3}{7}\)

Problem 3: Prove that for any events A and B, \(P(A) = P(A \mid B)P(B) + P(A \mid B^c)P(B^c)\).

Show Solution

Since \(\{B, B^c\}\) forms a partition of \(\Omega\), the result follows directly from the Law of Total Probability with \(n=2\).

📝 Summary: Key Takeaways

  • Law of Total Probability: \(P(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} P(A \mid B_i) P(B_i)\) for any partition \(\{B_1, \ldots, B_n\}\)
  • Partition: Events must be mutually exclusive, exhaustive, and have positive probability
  • Special Case (n = 2): \(P(A) = P(A \mid B)P(B) + P(A \mid B^c)P(B^c)\)
  • Countable Extension: \(P(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(A \mid B_i) P(B_i)\) for countable partitions
  • Continuous Extension: \(P(A) = \int P(A \mid X = x) f_X(x) dx\)
  • Applications: Medical testing, quality control, Bayesian inference, reliability analysis
