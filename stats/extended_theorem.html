📐 Extended Axiom of Addition & Axiom of Continuity

The equivalence of countable additivity and continuity of probability measures

Probability Theory · Unit II · Advanced Axioms
📜

1. Introduction

The Extended Axiom of Addition (also called Countable Additivity or σ-additivity) and the Axiom of Continuity are two fundamental properties of probability measures. While Kolmogorov's third axiom is Countable Additivity, the Continuity Axiom is a consequence that deals with limits of monotone sequences of events. This page proves that these two axioms are equivalent (given finite additivity).

We assume throughout that finite additivity holds (Axiom 3 for finitely many events). We then prove:

  • Countable Additivity ⇒ Continuity (and vice versa)
  • Both properties are essential for probability theory to handle infinite sequences of events
💡 Historical Note: Kolmogorov included Countable Additivity in his axioms. The Continuity Axiom is often stated separately in some texts as an alternative to Countable Additivity.
📚

2. Formal Definitions

📌 Definition 1: Extended Axiom of Addition (Countable Additivity)

A probability measure \(P\) satisfies the Extended Axiom of Addition if for any sequence of mutually exclusive events \(A_1, A_2, A_3, \ldots\) (i.e., \(A_i \cap A_j = \emptyset\) for \(i \neq j\)),

\[ P\left( \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i \right) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(A_i) \]

This is Kolmogorov's third axiom, extended to countably infinite collections.

📌 Definition 2: Axiom of Continuity

A probability measure \(P\) satisfies the Axiom of Continuity if for any monotone sequence of events:

  • Increasing sequence: If \(A_1 \subseteq A_2 \subseteq A_3 \subseteq \cdots\), then
    • \[ \lim_{n \to \infty} P(A_n) = P\left( \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n \right) \]
  • Decreasing sequence: If \(B_1 \supseteq B_2 \supseteq B_3 \supseteq \cdots\), then
    • \[ \lim_{n \to \infty} P(B_n) = P\left( \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n \right) \]
📌 Note: The decreasing case follows from the increasing case by complementation: \(A_n = B_n^c\) gives an increasing sequence.

4. Theorem 1: Countable Additivity Implies Continuity

📌 Statement

If a probability measure \(P\) satisfies the Extended Axiom of Addition (Countable Additivity), then it satisfies the Axiom of Continuity.

📝 Proof for Increasing Sequence:

Step 1 — Let \(A_1 \subseteq A_2 \subseteq A_3 \subseteq \cdots\) be a monotone increasing sequence of events.

Define \(B_1 = A_1\), and for \(n \geq 2\), define \(B_n = A_n \setminus A_{n-1} = A_n \cap A_{n-1}^c\).

Step 2 — Note that the \(B_n\) are mutually exclusive (disjoint):
\[ B_i \cap B_j = \emptyset \quad \text{for } i \neq j \]

Because \(B_n\) consists of points that first appear in \(A_n\).

Step 3 — Express \(A_n\) as the union of the first \(n\) \(B_i\)'s:
\[ A_n = \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} B_i \]
Step 4 — Express the union \(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n\) as the union of all \(B_i\)'s:
\[ \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n = \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} B_i \]
Step 5 — Apply finite additivity to \(A_n\):
\[ P(A_n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} P(B_i) \]
Step 6 — Apply Countable Additivity (Extended Axiom) to \(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} B_i\):
\[ P\left( \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} B_i \right) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(B_i) \]
Step 7 — Observe that \(\lim_{n \to \infty} P(A_n) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} P(B_i) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(B_i)\):
\[ \lim_{n \to \infty} P(A_n) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(B_i) = P\left( \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} B_i \right) = P\left( \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n \right) \]
Step 8 — Conclusion for increasing sequences:
\[ \boxed{\lim_{n \to \infty} P(A_n) = P\left( \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n \right)} \]

📝 Decreasing Sequence (by complementation):

Step 1 — Let \(B_1 \supseteq B_2 \supseteq B_3 \supseteq \cdots\) be a monotone decreasing sequence.
Step 2 — Define \(A_n = B_n^c\). Then \(A_1 \subseteq A_2 \subseteq A_3 \subseteq \cdots\) (increasing sequence).
Step 3 — Apply the increasing case to \(\{A_n\}\):
\[ \lim_{n \to \infty} P(A_n) = P\left( \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n \right) \]
Step 4 — Using the complement rule and De Morgan's laws:

\(P(A_n) = P(B_n^c) = 1 - P(B_n)\). Also, \(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n^c = \left( \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n \right)^c\).

Step 5 — Substitute into the limit equation:
\[ \lim_{n \to \infty} (1 - P(B_n)) = 1 - P\left( \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n \right) \]
Step 6 — Rearranging:
\[ 1 - \lim_{n \to \infty} P(B_n) = 1 - P\left( \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n \right) \]
\[ \lim_{n \to \infty} P(B_n) = P\left( \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n \right) \]
Step 7 — Conclusion for decreasing sequences:
\[ \boxed{\lim_{n \to \infty} P(B_n) = P\left( \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n \right)} \]
📌 Corollary 1.1 (Continuity from Above): For a decreasing sequence of events, the probability of the limit equals the limit of the probabilities.

📌 Corollary 1.2 (Continuity from Below): For an increasing sequence of events, the probability of the limit equals the limit of the probabilities.

📌 Corollary 1.3 (Application — Borel-Cantelli Lemma): Continuity is essential for proving the Borel-Cantelli lemmas and handling infinite sequences of events.
⬅️

5. Theorem 2: Continuity Implies Countable Additivity

📌 Statement

If a probability measure \(P\) satisfies the Axiom of Continuity (for both increasing and decreasing sequences) and finite additivity, then it satisfies the Extended Axiom of Addition (Countable Additivity).

📝 Proof:

Step 1 — Let \(A_1, A_2, A_3, \ldots\) be a sequence of mutually exclusive events:
\[ A_i \cap A_j = \emptyset \quad \text{for } i \neq j \]
Step 2 — Define the partial unions:
\[ S_n = \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} A_i \]

Note that \(S_1 \subseteq S_2 \subseteq S_3 \subseteq \cdots\) forms an increasing sequence of events.

Step 3 — Apply finite additivity to \(S_n\):

Since the \(A_i\) are mutually exclusive, by finite additivity:

\[ P(S_n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} P(A_i) \]
Step 4 — Observe that the union of all events is the limit of \(S_n\):
\[ \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} S_n \]
Step 5 — Apply the Continuity Axiom for increasing sequences:

Since \(S_n \uparrow \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i\) (monotone increasing), we have:

\[ \lim_{n \to \infty} P(S_n) = P\left( \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i \right) \]
Step 6 — Substitute the expression for \(P(S_n)\) from Step 3:
\[ \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} P(A_i) = P\left( \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i \right) \]
Step 7 — Recognize the left-hand side as the infinite series:
\[ \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(A_i) = P\left( \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i \right) \]
Step 8 — Conclusion:
\[ \boxed{P\left( \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i \right) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(A_i)} \]

Thus, Countable Additivity holds. ∎

📌 Corollary 2.1: Given finite additivity, Countable Additivity and Continuity are logically equivalent.

📌 Corollary 2.2 (Borel's First Lemma): For any decreasing sequence of events \(B_n\) with \(P(B_n) \to 0\), we have:
\[ P\left( \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n \right) = 0 \]
This follows from continuity.

📌 Corollary 2.3: Any finitely additive measure that fails continuity cannot be countably additive, and vice versa.
⚠️

6. Counterexample: Finitely Additive but not Continuous

It is possible to define a finitely additive probability measure on the natural numbers that is not continuous (and hence not countably additive).

Example (Banach Limit / Non-principal ultrafilter): Define \(P(A)\) as the asymptotic density of \(A\) if it exists; otherwise extend by a non-principal ultrafilter. This gives a finitely additive probability on \(\mathbb{N}\) where every finite set has probability 0, but \(P(\mathbb{N}) = 1\). For the decreasing sequence \(A_n = \{n, n+1, n+2, \ldots\}\), we have \(P(A_n) = 1\) for all \(n\), but \(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n = \emptyset\), so \(P(\emptyset) = 0 \neq \lim_{n \to \infty} P(A_n) = 1\). Thus, continuity fails.

🔄

7. Summary of the Equivalence

📌 Main Theorem (Equivalence of Axioms)

Assuming finite additivity, the following are equivalent for a probability measure \(P\):

  1. Extended Axiom of Addition (Countable Additivity): For any sequence of mutually exclusive events \(A_1, A_2, \ldots\),
    2. \[ P\left( \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i \right) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(A_i) \]
  2. Axiom of Continuity: For any monotone sequence of events,
    3. \[ \lim_{n \to \infty} P(A_n) = P\left( \lim_{n \to \infty} A_n \right) \]
    where \(\lim A_n = \bigcup A_n\) for increasing sequences and \(\bigcap A_n\) for decreasing sequences.

📝 Equivalence Diagram:

\[ \text{Countable Additivity} \quad \xrightarrow[\text{Theorem 1}]{\text{implies}} \quad \text{Continuity} \]
\[ \text{Continuity} \quad \xrightarrow[\text{Theorem 2}]{\text{implies}} \quad \text{Countable Additivity} \]
\[ \therefore \quad \boxed{\text{Countable Additivity} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{Continuity}} \]

(assuming Finite Additivity holds)

8. Consequences and Applications

📖 Application 1: Probability of a Countable Union
Countable additivity allows us to compute probabilities of infinite unions. For example, in coin tossing, the probability that a head eventually appears is:
\[ P(\text{head event}) = 1 - P(\text{no heads in any toss}) = 1 - \lim_{n \to \infty} \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n = 1 \]
This uses continuity.
📖 Application 2: Borel-Cantelli Lemma
If \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(A_n) < \infty\), then using continuity, we prove that \(P(\limsup A_n) = 0\), meaning infinitely many \(A_n\) occur with probability 0.

9. Practice Problems

Problem 1: Prove that if \(P\) is countably additive, then for any decreasing sequence \(B_1 \supseteq B_2 \supseteq B_3 \supseteq \cdots\), we have \(P(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n) = \lim_{n \to \infty} P(B_n)\).

Show Solution

Set \(A_n = B_n^c\). Then \(A_n\) is increasing. By continuity for increasing sequences, \(\lim P(A_n) = P(\bigcup A_n)\). By complement rule, \(\lim (1 - P(B_n)) = 1 - P(\bigcap B_n)\). Hence, \(\lim P(B_n) = P(\bigcap B_n)\).

Problem 2: Why does asymptotic density (limit of \(|A \cap \{1,\ldots,n\}|/n\)) fail to be countably additive?

Show Solution

Asymptotic density is only finitely additive. Consider \(A_n = \{n\}\). Then each \(A_n\) has density 0, but the union of all \(A_n\) is \(\mathbb{N}\), which has density 1. Thus, countable additivity fails, and continuity fails because the decreasing sequence \(B_n = \{n, n+1, \ldots\}\) has density 1 for all n, but the intersection is empty with density 0.

Problem 3: Given countable additivity, prove that \(P(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i) \leq \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(A_i)\) (Countable Boole's Inequality).

Show Solution

Let \(B_1 = A_1\), \(B_n = A_n \setminus \bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1} A_i\). Then \(B_n\) are mutually exclusive and \(\bigcup B_n = \bigcup A_n\). Also \(P(B_n) \leq P(A_n)\). By countable additivity, \(P(\bigcup A_n) = \sum P(B_n) \leq \sum P(A_n)\).

📝 Summary: Key Takeaways

  • Extended Axiom of Addition (Countable Additivity): \(P(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(A_i)\) for mutually exclusive events
  • Axiom of Continuity: For increasing sequences: \(\lim P(A_n) = P(\bigcup A_n)\); for decreasing sequences: \(\lim P(B_n) = P(\bigcap B_n)\)
  • Equivalence: Given finite additivity, Countable Additivity ⟺ Continuity
  • Proofs:
    • Countable Additivity ⇒ Continuity: Use disjoint decomposition of monotone sequences
    • Continuity ⇒ Countable Additivity: Use increasing partial sums and continuity from below
  • Importance: These axioms enable probability to handle infinite sample spaces and limit theorems (Law of Large Numbers, CLT)
