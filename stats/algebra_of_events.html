1. What is Algebra of Events?

The algebra of events is a branch of set theory applied to probability. It defines rules and operations for combining events (subsets of the sample space \( \Omega \)) to form new events.

The algebra of events follows the same laws as Boolean algebra. It provides a systematic way to:

  • Combine events using logical operators (AND, OR, NOT, XOR)
  • Simplify complex event expressions
  • Calculate probabilities of combined events
💡 Fundamental Principle: If \( A \) and \( B \) are events, then \( A \cup B \), \( A \cap B \), \( A - B \), \( A \triangle B \), and \( A^c \) are also events. This closure property makes the collection of events a sigma-algebra (or event space).

3. Derived Events and Their Formulas

Using the basic operations, we can create more complex events. Below are all possible combinations and when to use them.

📌 Event: "Exactly one of A or B occurs" (Symmetric Difference)

\[ A \triangle B = (A - B) \cup (B - A) = (A \cup B) - (A \cap B) \]
When to use: When you want the probability that A happens but not B, OR B happens but not A — i.e., exactly one event occurs, not both and not neither. This is the exclusive OR (XOR) operation.

📌 Event: "At most one of A or B occurs"

\[ (A^c \cap B^c) \cup (A \cap B^c) \cup (A^c \cap B) = \Omega - (A \cap B) \]
When to use: When you want the probability that 0 or 1 event occurs (i.e., not both). Equivalent to the complement of "both occur".

📌 Event: "At least two of A, B, C occur"

\[ (A \cap B) \cup (A \cap C) \cup (B \cap C) \]
When to use: For three events, when you want the probability that any two (or all three) occur. Used in inclusion-exclusion principle calculations.

📌 Event: "Exactly two of A, B, C occur"

\[ (A \cap B \cap C^c) \cup (A \cap B^c \cap C) \cup (A^c \cap B \cap C) \]
When to use: When you need precisely two events to occur, excluding the case where all three occur.

📌 Event: "None of A, B, C occur"

\[ A^c \cap B^c \cap C^c = (A \cup B \cup C)^c \]
When to use: When you want the probability that no event in a collection occurs. By De Morgan's law, this equals the complement of the union.

4. Fundamental Laws of Event Algebra

Identity Laws

\[ A \cup \emptyset = A \qquad A \cap \Omega = A \] \[ A \cup \Omega = \Omega \qquad A \cap \emptyset = \emptyset \]

Idempotent Laws

\[ A \cup A = A \qquad A \cap A = A \]

Complement Laws

\[ A \cup A^c = \Omega \qquad A \cap A^c = \emptyset \] \[ Involution Law: (A^c)^c = A \qquad \Omega^c = \emptyset,\ \emptyset^c = \Omega \]

Commutative Laws

\[ A \cup B = B \cup A \qquad A \cap B = B \cap A \]

Associative Laws

\[ (A \cup B) \cup C = A \cup (B \cup C) \] \[ (A \cap B) \cap C = A \cap (B \cap C) \]

Distributive Laws

\[ A \cap (B \cup C) = (A \cap B) \cup (A \cap C) \] \[ A \cup (B \cap C) = (A \cup B) \cap (A \cup C) \]

De Morgan's Laws

\[ (A \cup B)^c = A^c \cap B^c \] \[ (A \cap B)^c = A^c \cup B^c \]

Absorption Laws

\[ A \cup (A \cap B) = A \] \[ A \cap (A \cup B) = A \]

Difference Laws (Set Difference / Relative Complement)

\[ A - B = A \cap B^c \] \[ A - B = A - (A \cap B) \]

Interpretation: The difference removes elements of B from A. It is equivalent to the intersection of A with the complement of B.

Properties of Difference

\[ (A - B) \cap B = \emptyset \] \[ A - \emptyset = A \] \[ \emptyset - A = \emptyset \] \[ A - A = \emptyset \] \[ A - (B - C) = (A - B) \cup (A - C) \] \[(A \cup B) - C = (A - C) \cup (B - C) \] \[(A \cap B) \cup (A - B) = A \] \[(A \cap B) \cap (A - B) = \emptyset \] \[ A - (B \cup C) = (A - B) \cap (A - C) \] \[ A - (B \cap C) = (A - B) \cup (A - C) \]
When to use: These laws simplify expressions involving "A but not B." They are essential for converting difference operations into intersections and complements, enabling probability calculation via \( P(A - B) = P(A) - P(A \cap B) \).
📊

5. Probability Formulas for Combined Events

Addition Rule (Union)

\[ P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B) \]
When to use: To find the probability that at least one of A or B occurs.

Symmetric Difference

\[ P(A \triangle B) = P(A) + P(B) - 2P(A \cap B) \]
When to use: To find the probability that exactly one of A or B occurs.

Complement Rule

\[ P(A^c) = 1 - P(A) \]
When to use: When it's easier to calculate the probability that an event does NOT occur.

Difference Rule

\[ P(A - B) = P(A) - P(A \cap B) \]
When to use: To find the probability that A occurs but B does not.

Inclusion-Exclusion for Three Events

\[ P(A \cup B \cup C) = P(A) + P(B) + P(C) - P(A \cap B) - P(A \cap C) - P(B \cap C) + P(A \cap B \cap C) \]

6. Worked Examples

Example 1: Students Taking Courses
In a class of 100 students, 60 take Mathematics (M), 40 take Physics (P), and 20 take both. Find:
(a) \( P(M \cup P) \) — students taking at least one
(b) \( P(M - P) \) — students taking Math but not Physics
(c) \( P(M \triangle P) \) — students taking exactly one subject

Solution:
(a) \( P(M \cup P) = 0.6 + 0.4 - 0.2 = 0.8 \) (80 students)
(b) \( P(M - P) = 0.6 - 0.2 = 0.4 \) (40 students)
(c) \( P(M \triangle P) = 0.6 + 0.4 - 2(0.2) = 0.6 \) (60 students)
Example 2: Dice Roll
Roll a fair die. Let \( A = \{2,4,6\} \) (even), \( B = \{1,2,3\} \) (less than 4).
Find \( P(A \cup B) \), \( P(A \cap B) \), \( P(A - B) \), \( P(A \triangle B) \).

Solution:
\( A \cap B = \{2\} \), so \( P(A \cap B) = \frac{1}{6} \)
\( P(A \cup B) = \frac{3}{6} + \frac{3}{6} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{5}{6} \)
\( P(A - B) = \frac{3}{6} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3} \)
\( P(A \triangle B) = \frac{3}{6} + \frac{3}{6} - 2 \times \frac{1}{6} = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3} \)

📝 Summary: Key Takeaways

  • The algebra of events provides rules for combining events using union (\( \cup \)), intersection (\( \cap \)), complement (\( ^c \)), difference (\( - \)), and symmetric difference (\( \triangle \))
  • Symmetric difference \( A \triangle B \) represents exclusive OR — exactly one event occurs
  • De Morgan's Laws relate complements to unions and intersections
  • Venn diagrams provide intuitive visual representations of these operations
  • Probability formulas follow directly from set operations
